# Indonesian SMB Licensing and Compliance Reference

Last researched: 2026-11-01

Audience: AI business assistants (personas) serving Indonesian micro, small and medium businesses (UMKM): clinics, bakeries/F&B, retail, salons, laundries, online sellers, service firms. Read this before answering an owner's question about permits, halal, data protection, e-commerce or promotions.

How to use this file:

- Give the owner the rule, the next practical step and the official portal. Do not give a legal opinion.
- Items marked **[UNVERIFIED]** could not be confirmed from an official or reputable source during research. Say so to the owner and point them to the official channel.
- Regional rules (Perda/Perbup/Perwal) differ by kabupaten/kota. When a fee or procedure is set by the region, say so; do not quote one city's number as national.

## 1. Legal entity choices

### Rule

UMKM size is defined by **PP 7/2021** (implementing UU Cipta Kerja). Two measures are used:

- **Modal usaha** (business capital, excluding land and buildings), used at registration/establishment: Micro ≤ Rp1 billion; Small > Rp1 billion to Rp5 billion; Medium > Rp5 billion to Rp10 billion.
- **Hasil penjualan tahunan** (annual sales), used for government support programmes: Micro ≤ Rp2 billion; Small > Rp2 billion to Rp15 billion; Medium > Rp15 billion to Rp50 billion.

| Form                                                                 | Founders                                                                                           | Liability                                                                 | Capital                                                                                                                        | Set up via                                                                                                                                        | Typical fit                                                                          |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Usaha perorangan** (sole proprietor)                               | 1 person, using their NIK                                                                          | Unlimited: personal and business assets are not separated                 | None                                                                                                                           | NIB on oss.go.id directly, no notary                                                                                                              | Warung, home bakery, freelance service, starter online shop                          |
| **CV** (Persekutuan Komanditer)                                      | At least 2: active partner(s) (_sekutu komplementer_) and passive partner(s) (_sekutu komanditer_) | Active partners unlimited; passive partners limited to their contribution | No statutory minimum                                                                                                           | Notarial deed, registered by the notary in SABU (Kemenkum). Not a legal entity (_badan usaha_, not _badan hukum_)                                 | Small family or partner business that wants a "company" name without PT formalities  |
| **PT Perorangan** (single-person PT; UU Cipta Kerja + **PP 8/2021**) | 1 Indonesian citizen (WNI), age 17 or over, legally competent                                      | Limited (legal entity)                                                    | Set by the founder (PP 8/2021 art. 3). At least 25% must be issued and paid up, with proof filed electronically within 60 days | _Pernyataan Pendirian_ filled online at AHU Online (ahu.go.id), **no notary**; the electronic registration certificate grants legal-entity status | Micro or small owner who wants limited liability cheaply                             |
| **PT** (persekutuan modal)                                           | At least 2 founders/shareholders                                                                   | Limited                                                                   | Set by the founders' agreement (PP 8/2021 art. 3), 25% paid up; higher minimums apply only where a sector rule requires them   | Notarial deed + AHU approval (SK Kemenkum)                                                                                                        | Growing business, outside investors, medium or large scale, foreign investment (PMA) |

PT Perorangan specifics (PP 8/2021):

- Must convert to a regular PT by notarial deed if (a) it gets more than one shareholder, or (b) it no longer meets the micro/small criteria (art. 9).
- Must file an annual financial report (balance sheet, profit and loss, notes) electronically to the Minister **within 6 months after the end of the accounting period** (art. 10). Failure brings administrative sanctions (art. 12).
- PNBP (government fee) for establishment is reported as **Rp50,000**, paid within 7 days of ordering the voucher on AHU. **[UNVERIFIED against the current PNBP schedule]**
- "One person may found only one PT Perorangan per year" is a commonly cited rule, attributed to Permenkumham 21/2021. **[UNVERIFIED]**: not found in the PP 8/2021 text.

### When to choose which (guidance to give the owner)

- Testing an idea or very small home business: usaha perorangan with NIB. Fastest and free.
- Wants a separate legal entity, is alone and micro/small: PT Perorangan.
- Has a partner, wants simplicity, accepts unlimited liability for the active partner: CV. A PT is generally the better choice now that PT costs have fallen.
- Expects investors, a bank loan or a tender, has more than one owner, or is medium scale or above: PT. Use a notaris.
- Clinics: check the sector rule. Permenkes 11/2025 sets which entity types can hold a clinic permit. **[UNVERIFIED for each clinic type; ask the Dinas Kesehatan / DPMPTSP]**
- Notary and consultant fees vary widely; tell the owner to get quotes. Do not quote a national price.

## 2. OSS-RBA: NIB, risk levels, KBLI, LKPM

### Legal basis

- UU 6/2023 (Cipta Kerja).
- **PP 28/2025** on Penyelenggaraan Perizinan Berusaha Berbasis Risiko (replaces PP 5/2021). Set 5 Jun 2025; OSS adjusted with full implementation from **5 Oct 2025**.
- Peraturan Menteri Investasi dan Hilirisasi/Kepala BKPM **5/2025** (procedures, LKPM).
- KBLI 2025: **Peraturan BPS 7/2025** (in force 18 Dec 2025; revokes Peraturan BPS 2/2020 on KBLI 2020).

### Risk levels and what each requires

| Risk level                        | What the owner gets / needs                                                                                                                         |
| --------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Rendah** (low)                  | **NIB** only. For UMK, NIB is the single licence and also covers SNI/halal commitments _where applicable_ (it does not replace a halal certificate) |
| **Menengah rendah** (medium-low)  | NIB + **Sertifikat Standar** issued automatically on the owner's self-declaration (_pernyataan mandiri_) in OSS; no prior verification              |
| **Menengah tinggi** (medium-high) | NIB + **Sertifikat Standar verified** by the competent ministry or regional office before it becomes effective (e.g. klinik pratama 86105)          |
| **Tinggi** (high)                 | NIB + **Izin** (licence) issued after verification, plus sector requirements                                                                        |

Plus _persyaratan dasar_ (basic requirements) where applicable: **KKPR** (spatial conformity), **Persetujuan Lingkungan** (environmental approval) and **PBG/SLF** (building). For UMK, KKPR can be met by a **self-declaration** at NIB registration that the location complies with the spatial plan. Low-risk activities get an NIB that also serves as the SPPL. Separate supporting permits (**PB UMKU**) cover things like SPP-IRT, halal and product registrations.

PP 28/2025 adds:

- **SLAs** at every stage and **fiktif positif**: if the agency does not decide within the SLA, OSS issues the licence automatically.
- Administrative sanctions processed through OSS.

### Procedure (new business, typical)

1. Create an OSS account at **oss.go.id**: NIK for an individual, or company data from AHU for a PT/CV. Micro owners can use the UMK flow.
2. Enter business data, location and **KBLI 2025** codes (one per activity).
3. OSS shows the risk level per KBLI and the requirements.
4. Complete the self-declarations (KKPR for UMK, environmental SPPL), then any Sertifikat Standar or Izin steps.
5. Download the NIB, then apply for supporting permits (PB UMKU, e.g. SPP-IRT or halal) as needed.
6. Calendar the LKPM reporting obligations (below).

### KBLI 2025: codes commonly used by SMBs (verified on the official oss.go.id conversion pages)

Existing permits issued under KBLI 2020 **remain valid**. Codes are converted automatically unless the business activity itself changed (BPS, 27 Apr 2026). New registrations use KBLI 2025. Always confirm the description fits the activity in OSS; the conversion pages say they are reference material, not a legal ruling.

### LKPM (Laporan Kegiatan Penanaman Modal)

- **Micro businesses are exempt.**
- **Small**: every semester. **Medium and large**: every quarter. One report per business location/NIB project, filed in OSS.
- **Deadline**: Ortax (citing Permen Investasi/BKPM 5/2025) reports the deadline moved from the **10th to the 15th** of the month after the period: Sem I by 15 Jul, Sem II by 15 Jan; quarterly by 15 Apr, 15 Jul, 15 Oct, 15 Jan. Some consultants still cite the 10th. **Tell owners to file by the 10th to be safe**, and treat the 15th as the official deadline as reported by Ortax. **[UNVERIFIED against the Permen text]**
- Sanctions are tiered administrative sanctions. Reported trigger: failing to report for two consecutive periods. Escalation can reach suspension/revocation of the permit.

## 3. Food businesses: SPP-IRT, BPOM MD/ML, labelling, halal

### 3a. SPP-IRT (PIRT): home-industry processed food

**Legal basis:** UU 18/2012 (Pangan); PP 86/2019 (Keamanan Pangan); **Peraturan BPOM 4/2024** (guidelines for SPP-IRT; revokes Per BPOM 22/2018).

Rules:

- Required for certain processed foods made by a home industry (IRTP) and sold in **retail packaging** (art. 3).
- **Not required** for: food with a shelf life under 7 days, ready-to-eat food (_pangan siap saji_, e.g. restaurant meals, fresh cakes sold same day), and unprocessed food.
- **Cannot be used** (BPOM MD needed instead) for: food additives (BTP), processing aids, foods subject to mandatory SNI, products needing special handling (e.g. cold chain), **products carrying claims**, special medical foods (PKGK), alcoholic drinks, non-food (art. 10). Only product types listed in Annex I qualify.
- Issued by the **Bupati/Wali Kota** through OSS, integrated with sppirt.pom.go.id.
- **Valid 5 years** (art. 22). Renew no later than 1 month before expiry. Expired SPP-IRT means the product may not be distributed.
- Post-issuance commitments (art. 15): attend **Penyuluhan Keamanan Pangan (PKP)** and pass with a score of at least 60 (certificate from Dinkes Kab/Kota or BPOM); meet hygiene and good-production practice; meet labelling rules; meet safety and quality limits (additives, contaminants). Unmet commitments can lead to freezing or cancellation.
- The PIRT number has at least 15 digits; the last two digits show the expiry year.
- Fees: PKP fees and any local charges vary by region. **[UNVERIFIED: no national fee confirmed]**

Procedure:

1. NIB (KBLI 10xxx production code).
2. In OSS, apply for SPP-IRT for each product (name, composition, process, label draft, photos).
3. SPP-IRT is issued (the application can be suspended up to 3 months for missing data, then rejected).
4. Complete PKP, hygiene and label commitments, and keep evidence for Dinkes inspection.

### 3b. BPOM MD / ML (izin edar)

- Domestic processed food that does not qualify for SPP-IRT needs **BPOM RI MD**; imported food needs **BPOM RI ML**. Registration is through BPOM's e-registration system.
- Usually requires a production-facility inspection/assessment (CPPOB/GMP), label approval and product testing. **[UNVERIFIED: current fee tariff (PP on BPOM PNBP) not checked]**

### 3c. Labelling (processed food in retail packaging)

**Legal basis:** Peraturan BPOM 31/2018 on Label Pangan Olahan (amended, most recently by **Per BPOM 6/2024**).

Minimum label content: product name, ingredient list, net weight/volume, name and address of producer/importer, halal logo where certified, production date/code, expiry date (_baik digunakan sebelum_), and the registration number (PIRT or MD/ML). Use Bahasa Indonesia.

Claims: Per BPOM 1/2022 (claims on label and in advertising) and Per BPOM 6/2021 (food advertising) **prohibit claims that a food prevents, treats or cures disease**. Claims must match the approved label. PIRT products may not carry claims.

### 3d. Halal certification

**Legal basis:** UU 33/2014 (JPH) as amended by UU 6/2023; PP 39/2021 → **PP 42/2024**, amended by **PP 40/2026** (14 Oct 2026); Peraturan BPJPH 2/2026 (sanctions); Kepkaban BPJPH 146/2025 (self-declare technical guidance).

**Current mandatory deadlines (after PP 40/2026):**

| Who / what                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    | Deadline                                            |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| Food, drinks, slaughtered products, slaughter services: **medium and large** businesses                                                                                                                                                       | **Already mandatory since 18 Oct 2024** (unchanged) |
| Food, drinks, slaughtered products, slaughter services: **micro and small (UMK)**                                                                                                                                                             | Postponed from 17 Oct 2026 to **17 Oct 2028**       |
| Food and drink-related services (other than slaughter) from medium/large businesses                                                                                                                                                           | 17 Oct 2028 (BPJPH)                                 |
| Imported food, drinks and slaughtered products                                                                                                                                                                                                | From 18 Oct 2028 (pending mutual-recognition deals) |
| Cosmetics, traditional medicine, quasi-drugs, health supplements, chemical products, GMO products, clothing/headwear/accessories, household health supplies and equipment, worship items, stationery/office supplies, Class A medical devices | 17 Oct 2028                                         |
| OTC and limited-OTC drugs, Class B medical devices                                                                                                                                                                                            | 17 Oct 2029                                         |
| Prescription drugs (except psychotropics), Class C medical devices                                                                                                                                                                            | 17 Oct 2034                                         |

What this means now (Nov 2026):

- A **UMK bakery or warung is not yet sanctionable** for lacking a halal certificate until 17 Oct 2028. Still advise getting one: the 2026 free quota exists, and buyers and marketplaces ask for it.
- Products containing **non-halal (haram) ingredients must say so** on the label. This applies regardless of the postponement.
- A **medium or large** F&B business without a halal certificate (unless it labels as non-halal) is out of compliance today.
- Halal certificates no longer expire every 4 years. Since UU 6/2023, they stay valid as long as the ingredients and process do not change; changes must be reported.

**Sanctions (Peraturan BPJPH 2/2026, in force 5 Jun 2026):** written warning, administrative fines, revocation of the halal certificate, withdrawal of products from circulation, suspension of operations, cancellation of registration numbers. It covers failure to certify, failure to keep halal status, missing halal label, and not reporting changes to ingredients or process. It is proportional, with time to correct, and objections can be filed with BPJPH. Some secondary sources say fines go up to Rp2 billion. **[UNVERIFIED: BPJPH's own announcement does not state amounts]**

**Routes and costs:**

- **Self-declare (pernyataan pelaku usaha) / SEHATI (free)**: for UMK with simple, low-risk products using ingredients already confirmed halal, assisted by a **Pendamping PPH** (halal process assistant), with a fatwa from the Komite Fatwa. 2026 quota: **1.35 million free certificates** (BPJPH). Kepkaban 146/2025 added warung culinary (Warteg, Warung Nasi Sunda, ayam goreng and similar).
  - Reported criteria under Kepkaban 146/2025 (UKM Indonesia summary): NIB as micro/small; halal-confirmed materials; simple manual or semi-automatic process; no haram materials; **annual revenue ≤ Rp15 billion**, which supersedes the older Rp500 million cap often quoted; at most one production facility and one outlet; equipment separated from non-halal equipment; meat only from halal-certified sources/RPH; simple preservation. **[UNVERIFIED in the Kepkaban text itself]**
  - Products with meat from non-certified sources, complex processes or high-risk ingredients must use the regular route.
- **Regular route**: via an LPH (halal inspection body) audit, then the fatwa, then a certificate from BPJPH. Reported UMK tariff: **Rp650,000** (Rp300,000 registration and determination + Rp350,000 LPH inspection) under Kepkaban 141/2021. **[UNVERIFIED that this tariff is still current in 2026]** Medium and large business fees are higher and depend on the LPH.
- Apply at **ptsp.halal.go.id (SIHALAL)**. NIB required.

Procedure (self-declare):

1. Make sure you have an NIB with the right KBLI.
2. Find a Pendamping PPH (through BPJPH/SIHALAL partner institutions).
3. Prepare the list of ingredients with halal evidence, the process flow, product names/photos, the Halal Assurance System (SJPH) manual and the name of the halal supervisor.
4. Submit on SIHALAL with the self-declare pledge; the Pendamping PPH verifies on site.
5. Komite Fatwa decision, then BPJPH issues the certificate. Display the national halal logo per BPJPH rules.

### 3e. Clinics (klinik pratama, dental clinics): sector notes

## 4. Personal data protection (UU 27/2022 PDP)

### Status (Nov 2026)

- UU 27/2022 enacted 17 Oct 2022; **fully applicable since 17 Oct 2024** (end of the 2-year transition).
- **PP 33/2026** (implementing regulation): promulgated mid-July 2026, **in force mid-January 2027**. Hukumonline gives 15 Jul 2026 → 15 Jan 2027; other sources give 16 Jul → 16 Jan. It has 225 articles in 12 chapters covering data-subject rights, DPIA, DPO, cross-border transfer, breach handling and the administrative-sanction procedure. About 20 provisions defer to future technical rules from the Agency.
- **Lembaga PDP (supervisory agency): not yet established** as of Oct 2026. The draft Perpres was submitted 20 May 2026 and Komdigi said it is in the final stage; Komdigi performs supervision in the interim. **Enforcement of administrative sanctions depends on the Agency being operational**, but criminal provisions and civil claims already apply.
- Constitutional Court decision **151/PUU-XXII/2024** (30 Jul 2025) reads art. 53(1) "dan" as "**dan/atau**": a DPO is required if **any one** of the art. 53 triggers applies.

### What an SMB that keeps customer/patient data must do

1. **Have a lawful basis** (art. 20) for each use: explicit consent, contract, legal obligation, vital interest, public task or legitimate interest. Under PP 33/2026, legitimate interest needs a written, documented balancing analysis.
2. **Consent**: explicit and recorded (written or electronically recorded), for a specific stated purpose, and **separate from general terms and conditions** (PP 33/2026). Withdrawal must be as easy as giving consent. Children's data needs parent/guardian consent.
3. **Purpose limitation and minimisation**: collect only what is needed, use it only for the stated purpose, keep it only as long as needed, then delete or anonymise it.
4. **Specific (sensitive) data** includes health and medical records, biometric, genetic, criminal records, children's data and personal financial data. Clinics, salons recording treatments and pharmacies handle this, so stricter care applies.
5. **Security**: access control, passwords/2FA, encryption where feasible, and no patient or customer lists in personal WhatsApp groups or public spreadsheets.
6. **Data-subject rights**: access, correction, deletion, withdrawal of consent, objection to automated decisions. Respond within the statutory timeline (UU PDP generally 3×24 hours for many requests). **[UNVERIFIED for each right; check PP 33/2026]**
7. **Breach notification** (art. 46): notify the **data subjects and the Lembaga** in writing **within 3×24 hours** (72 hours), stating what data, when and how, and the mitigation. Some commentators read the clock as starting when the breach is confirmed; PP 33/2026 details this. In some cases the public must also be notified.
8. **DPO / PPDP** (art. 53, as read by the MK): required if processing is for public services, **or** the core activity involves large-scale, regular and systematic monitoring, **or** the core activity is large-scale processing of specific data. A busy clinic chain or a large loyalty programme may qualify; a single small warung usually will not. The DPO can be internal or external.
9. **DPIA** before high-risk processing (art. 34), including specific data at scale, profiling and automated decisions. PP 33/2026 explicitly mentions AI, machine learning, smart technology and IoT. **This matters for AI chat assistants handling customer data.**
10. **Processors** (e.g. a POS, CRM or AI vendor) need a written agreement. The controller stays responsible.
11. **Cross-border transfer** only to countries with adequate protection, or with appropriate safeguards, or with consent (art. 56).
12. Keep a **record of processing activities**.

### Sanctions

- **Administrative** (art. 57): written warning, temporary suspension of processing, deletion/destruction of data, and fines **up to 2% of annual revenue/receipts** per violation variable. Under PP 33/2026, decisions are published by the Agency and sanctions can be applied without a prior warning. Business scale and ability to pay are mitigating factors; **there is no small-business exemption**.
- **Criminal** (art. 67–70), for intentional unlawful acts: obtaining/collecting data unlawfully, up to 5 years and/or Rp5 billion; unlawful disclosure, up to 4 years and/or Rp4 billion; unlawful use, up to 5 years and/or Rp5 billion; creating false data, up to 6 years and/or Rp6 billion. Corporations face fines up to 10× plus additional penalties. **[UNVERIFIED: article-by-article amounts recalled from the UU text; confirm on BPK]**

## 5. Consumer protection, e-commerce, payments

### Legal basis

UU 8/1999 (Perlindungan Konsumen); PP 80/2019 (PMSE / e-commerce); **Permendag 19/2026** (replaces Permendag 31/2023); Permendag 35/2013 (price display); UU 7/2011 (Mata Uang); BI QRIS policy.

### Rules

- **Consumer rights and seller duties (UU 8/1999 art. 4, 7)**: correct, clear and honest information; goods as promised; compensation or replacement when goods or services are not as agreed or are defective.
- **Banned standard clauses (art. 18)**: e.g. "_barang yang sudah dibeli tidak dapat dikembalikan_" (goods bought cannot be returned) or refusing refunds. Such clauses are void, and breach can be punished **up to 5 years or Rp2 billion** (art. 62). Owners should remove such notices from receipts and websites and instead set a fair return policy.
- **Price display (Permendag 35/2013)**: prices of goods and service tariffs must be shown clearly, legibly, in Rupiah, and must state whether tax or other charges are included.
- **Receipts**: no general standalone "must issue a receipt" rule for all SMBs was confirmed. Restaurants and similar are usually required by regional tax rules (PBJT under UU 1/2022 HKPD) to issue bills, and PKP-registered businesses must issue tax invoices. **[UNVERIFIED: varies by region and tax status]**
- **E-commerce returns (PP 80/2019)**: sellers must give **at least 2 working days** from receipt to exchange or cancel when goods are late, wrong, have hidden defects, are damaged or are expired. Sellers must also provide accurate identity, product, price and terms information.
- **Permendag 19/2026** (signed 8 Jun 2026):
  - Online sellers must hold a business permit, **at least an NIB**.
  - Marketplaces must reject new sellers without one. Existing sellers get up to **6 months** from platform registration to complete it, shown as "Dalam Proses Legalisasi"; after that, suspension.
  - Platforms must disclose fees; sellers can contest unjustified fee increases within 14 working days.
  - Local and UMKM products get priority placement.
  - **US$100 FOB minimum price** per unit for cross-border imported finished goods (unless exempted).
  - **Social commerce may not process payments** or act as producers.
  - Anti-predatory pricing.
  - Official-store labels need proof.
  - **AI-generated content must be labelled.** This applies to AI-written product descriptions and images that personas may help create.
  - Platforms must run complaint channels.
  - Government says no new burdens for UMKM beyond legalisation.
  - Effective date and the exact treatment of pure WhatsApp/Instagram sellers who do not use a platform were not confirmed. **[UNVERIFIED]**
- **Cash and QRIS**:
  - Refusing **Rupiah cash** is prohibited (UU 7/2011 art. 21; criminal penalty up to 1 year and fine up to Rp200 million, art. 33). A "QRIS only / no cash" policy is **not lawful**. Shops must still accept cash. (BI statements and the Dec 2025 Roti'O case.)
  - There is **no general obligation to accept QRIS**.
  - If you do accept QRIS: MDR is 0% for micro merchants on transactions ≤ Rp500,000 (0.3% above that), and 0% for all merchant classes on transactions ≤ Rp100,000 (0.7% above that) from 1 Oct 2026. **MDR may not be passed on to the buyer.**

## 6. Advertising and promotions

### Prize draws: Undian Gratis Berhadiah (UGB)

**Legal basis:** UU 22/1954 (Undian); **Permensos 3/2024** on Penyelenggaraan UGB (revokes Permensos 4/2021).

- Any **free prize draw** tied to buying or using a product, i.e. one with an organiser, a promoted product, fixed limited prizes, unlimited participants, a limited period and a chance element, **needs a permit from the Minister of Social Affairs**. This covers direct (scratch cards, coupons in packs) and indirect (draw on a set date by coupon, unique code, phone number, points) draws, offline or online.
- Process: application via the Kemensos licensing app → provincial verification and recommendation → Kemensos verification → approval → payment → promotion permit → permit issued.
- Costs: the application/permit fee **plus a mandatory domestic grant of at least 10% of the total prize value**, non-refundable (art. 24).
- **Not permitted** for promoting medicines, traditional medicine and supplements, medical devices and **health services** (so a clinic cannot run a prize draw), infant formula under 1 year, cigarettes and alcohol.
- Applicant documents include NIB/permits, NPWP, the director's KTP, PBB or a lease, and a statement guaranteeing participants' data confidentiality under PDP.
- Sanctions: written warning up to permit revocation; unlicensed lotteries can be criminal under UU 22/1954.
- Interpretation: promotions with no chance element (e.g. guaranteed gift with purchase, "buy 2 get 1", discounts) do not meet the UGB elements and generally need no Kemensos permit. **[UNVERIFIED: this is an inference from the art. 2 elements, not an official statement]** Quizzes or contests judged on skill are a grey area; advise checking with Kemensos.

### Health and beauty claims

- **Processed food**: no claims that food prevents, treats or cures disease (Per BPOM 1/2022, 6/2021). Claims must match the BPOM-approved label. PIRT products may not carry claims.
- **Health services (clinics)**: Permenkes **1787/MENKES/PER/XII/2010** on health-service advertising and publicity (still listed as in force) requires ads to be informative, educational and not misleading. It **prohibits patient testimonials** and sales promotions such as discounts or gifts for medical services, and bars superlative or "guaranteed result" claims. Before/after photos used as advertising are commonly cited as violations. Whether newer rules under UU 17/2023 / PP 28/2024 have changed this is **[UNVERIFIED]**.
- **Cosmetics/salons**: cosmetic product claims are regulated by BPOM (no medical claims for cosmetics). Salon treatments must not be marketed as medical procedures. **[UNVERIFIED: specific current BPOM cosmetic-claim regulation number not checked]**
- **AI content**: under Permendag 19/2026, label AI-generated content on e-commerce platforms.

## 8. Local permits that still matter

- **PBG (Persetujuan Bangunan Gedung)** replaced IMB (UU Cipta Kerja; **PP 16/2021**). It is needed to build or alter a building, applied for through **SIMBG** (simbg.pu.go.id) at the regency/city. The **retribusi** (fee) is set by each region's Perda using an integrated index formula.
- **SLF (Sertifikat Laik Fungsi)**: certificate that a building is fit for use. Required for buildings used for business, especially public-facing ones (clinics, restaurants, shops), and before some sector permits (clinics: PBG + SLF are prerequisites). Existing buildings without IMB/PBG may need to regularise through SLF/PBG procedures. **[UNVERIFIED: details vary by region]**
- **Surat Keterangan Domisili Usaha (SKDU) and SITU: abolished.** Regions were instructed to stop issuing them (SE Mendagri 503/2019, which treats them as _izin gangguan_). Under PBBR, the location question is handled by KKPR, environmental approval and PBG/SLF in OSS. Some banks or landlords still ask for a domicile letter; that is a private requirement, not a licence, and a _kelurahan_ certificate or a lease can satisfy it.
- **Izin gangguan (HO)**: abolished (Permendagri 19/2017 revoked the guidelines). **[UNVERIFIED: revoking instrument number recalled, not re-checked]**
- **Regional trade permits**: the old SIUP/TDP were absorbed into the NIB. Some regions still require sector-specific local recommendations (e.g. modern-store location rules near traditional markets, alcohol sales permits, entertainment venues). Check with the local DPMPTSP.
- **Regional taxes affecting SMBs**: PBJT (restaurant tax) for restaurants and cafés above the regional revenue threshold; reklame tax. Rates and thresholds are set by each Perda.

## 9. What an assistant must never do

1. **Never submit, sign or click "agree" on any government application on the owner's behalf**: OSS, AHU, SIHALAL, SPP-IRT, BPOM, SIMBG, Kemensos UGB, tax. Never ask for or store the owner's OSS/AHU/SIHALAL passwords, OTPs or e-signature PINs. Prepare checklists and draft texts; the owner submits.
2. **Never make self-declarations for the owner** (KKPR, SPPL, Sertifikat Standar self-declare, halal self-declare pledge, UMK revenue statement). These are legal statements by the owner, and false statements carry sanctions.
3. **Never state that a business is "legal", "licensed" or "halal"** unless the owner shows the document (NIB, Sertifikat Standar, SPP-IRT, halal certificate). Never put a halal logo, PIRT number or BPOM number on a design unless the owner has that exact number.
4. **Never invent KBLI codes, fees, deadlines or regulation numbers.** If unsure, say so and point to oss.go.id, halal.go.id, pom.go.id or the local DPMPTSP / Dinkes.
5. **Never draft health claims** ("cures", "prevents", "guaranteed results"), patient testimonials or clinic discount ads; never draft prize draws without telling the owner about the Kemensos permit.
6. **Never write "goods cannot be returned" clauses** or "cashless only / no cash" notices.
7. **Never move customer or patient personal data** to unapproved tools or groups, and never use it for a purpose the customer did not consent to. Flag possible data breaches immediately and remind the owner of the 3×24-hour notification duty.
8. **Recommend a professional** when needed:
   - **Notaris** for a PT or CV deed, conversion of a PT Perorangan to a PT, land/lease deeds.
   - **Konsultan perizinan / lawyer** for medium-high or high-risk permits (clinics), UKL-UPL, PMA, disputes and sanctions.
   - **Tax consultant (konsultan pajak)** for PKP, PBJT and PPh questions.
   - **LPH / Pendamping PPH** for halal.
   - **A DPO or privacy lawyer** for clinics or large customer databases, and for DPIAs on AI use.
9. Label AI-generated marketing content where Permendag 19/2026 requires it (e-commerce).
